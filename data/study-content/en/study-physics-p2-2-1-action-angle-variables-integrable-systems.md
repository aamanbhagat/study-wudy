## 1. The one-sentence answer
**Action-angle variables are the canonical coordinates in which every bounded orbit of a Liouville-integrable Hamiltonian system becomes uniform rotation on a torus, with the action variables constant and the angle variables linear in time.**

In an integrable system the phase space is foliated by invariant tori. Each torus is labelled by a set of constants of motion whose values fix the “size” of the orbit. The action variables are simply the areas enclosed by the independent cycles on that torus; because they are constants they serve as new momenta. Their conjugate angles advance at constant rates fixed by the frequencies of the motion. The transformation therefore converts the original, possibly complicated, differential equations into the trivial set \(\dot{J}_i=0\), \(\dot{\theta}_i=\omega_i(J)\).

The construction works only when the motion is quasi-periodic and the frequencies are incommensurate; otherwise the orbit is not dense on the torus and the angle variables are not well-defined globally. When the frequencies become commensurate, resonances appear and the torus degenerates.

> [!NOTE]
> The single most powerful consequence is that the time evolution of any observable is obtained by a Fourier series in the angles whose coefficients depend only on the actions; perturbation theory then reduces to algebraic manipulation of those coefficients.

## 2. Why this matters — concrete and current
Spacecraft attitude dynamics for rigid-body satellites (e.g., the Hubble or James Webb telescopes) are routinely cast into action-angle form so that small torques from solar radiation pressure or reaction wheels can be treated by averaging over the fast angles, yielding secular drift equations that mission planners integrate for years.

In accelerator physics the betatron motion of particles in a synchrotron is described by action-angle variables; CERN’s MAD-X code and Fermilab’s Synergia both use these coordinates to compute tune shifts and resonance widths that limit beam lifetime.

The three-body problem in celestial mechanics, after reduction to Delaunay or Poincaré action-angle elements, supplies the starting point for the Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem; modern long-term integrations of the outer planets (Laskar’s work at Observatoire de Paris) rely on these variables to detect the chaotic diffusion that may destabilise Mercury on gigayear timescales.

In semiclassical quantum mechanics the Einstein–Brillouin–Keller quantisation condition states that each action variable equals an integer multiple of \(\hbar\); this supplies the leading-order spectrum of the hydrogen atom and of vibrational modes in polyatomic molecules studied by high-resolution infrared spectroscopy.

## 4. Building the idea — from intuition to formalism

### Step 1 — Constants label the torus
A Liouville-integrable system possesses \(n\) independent constants \(F_i\) that are in involution. Each regular level set \(F_i = c_i\) is a compact, connected \(n\)-torus.

**Example.** The isotropic harmonic oscillator in two dimensions has constants \(H\) and \(L_z\); their common level sets are circles (1-tori) in the four-dimensional phase space.

Formally, the map \(\mathbf{F}: \mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^n\) is a submersion on the level set and the Hamiltonian vector fields \(X_{F_i}\) are tangent to it and linearly independent.

> [!WARNING]
> If the constants fail to be independent at even one point, the level set may drop dimension and the torus picture collapses.

### Step 2 — Choose a basis of cycles
On each torus select \(n\) independent, closed curves \(\gamma_i\) that generate the first homology group \(H_1(\mathbb{T}^n,\mathbb{Z})\). These cycles are the “directions” around the torus.

**Example.** For a two-degree-of-freedom system the two cycles can be the orbit of one oscillator with the other frozen, and vice versa.

### Step 3 — Define the actions as enclosed areas
The action variables are the normalised symplectic areas
\[
J_i = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\gamma_i}\mathbf{p}\cdot d\mathbf{q}.
\]
Because the \(\gamma_i\) lie on a level set of the \(F_k\), each \(J_i\) is constant along the motion.

### Step 4 — Construct the generating function
The type-2 generating function \(S(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{J})\) is obtained by integrating the closed one-form \(\mathbf{p}\cdot d\mathbf{q}\) from a fixed base point to \(\mathbf{q}\) along any path on the torus; its differential yields the old momenta and the new angles
\[
\theta_i = \frac{\partial S}{\partial J_i}.
\]

### Step 5 — The transformed Hamiltonian depends only on the actions
After the canonical transformation the new Hamiltonian is \(K(\mathbf{J})\). Hamilton’s equations therefore become
\[
\dot{J}_i=0,\qquad\dot{\theta}_i=\frac{\partial K}{\partial J_i}=\omega_i(\mathbf{J}).
\]
The angles advance uniformly; the frequencies \(\omega_i\) are constant on each torus.

### Step 6 — Frequencies and resonance condition
The motion is quasi-periodic with frequencies \(\omega_i\). When a linear combination \(m\cdot\omega=0\) with integer vector \(m\neq0\), a resonance occurs and the torus is foliated by lower-dimensional tori.

### Step 7 — Textbook statement
For a Liouville-integrable system on a compact energy surface the phase space is foliated by invariant tori on which the flow is linear in suitable angle coordinates conjugate to the action variables defined by the period integrals above (Arnold, *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics*, §49).

## 5. Worked examples — every step shown

**Example 1 — One-dimensional harmonic oscillator**  
*Given:* \(H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac12 m\omega^2 q^2\).  
*Find:* \(J\) and \(\theta\).

The single cycle is the closed orbit itself.  
\[
J=\frac1{2\pi}\oint p\,dq=\frac{E}{\omega}.
\]
*Why:* The integral equals the area of the ellipse divided by \(2\pi\).  
The angle is \(\theta=\omega t+\theta_0\).  
**Final answer**  
\[J=\frac{E}{\omega},\qquad\theta=\omega t+\theta_0.\]

*Reflection:* The action is simply the energy scaled by the period; the example is trivial yet reveals that \(J\) is an adiabatic invariant.

**Example 2 — Planar Kepler problem**  
*Given:* \(H=\frac{p_r^2}{2m}+\frac{p_\phi^2}{2mr^2}-\frac{k}{r}\).  
*Find:* Delaunay actions \(J_r,J_\phi\).

Cycle \(\gamma_\phi\): full \(2\pi\) rotation at fixed \(r\).  
\[
J_\phi=\frac1{2\pi}\oint p_\phi\,d\phi=p_\phi=L.
\]
Cycle \(\gamma_r\): radial oscillation between turning points.  
\[
J_r=\frac1{2\pi}\oint p_r\,dr=\sqrt{-2mE}\,a-L,
\]
where \(a=-k/(2E)\).  
**Final answer**  
\[J_\phi=L,\qquad J_r=\sqrt{-2mE}\,a-L.\]

*Reflection:* The combination \(J_r+J_\phi\) is the total action whose frequency gives the Kepler period; resonance \(\omega_r=\omega_\phi\) appears automatically.

**Example 3 — Anisotropic oscillator with incommensurate frequencies**  
*Given:* \(H=\frac{p_x^2}{2}+\frac12\omega_x^2 x^2+\frac{p_y^2}{2}+\frac12\omega_y^2 y^2\), \(\omega_x/\omega_y\) irrational.  
*Find:* Actions.

Separate cycles give  
\[
J_x=\frac{E_x}{\omega_x},\qquad J_y=\frac{E_y}{\omega_y}.
\]
Angles advance as \(\theta_x=\omega_x t\), \(\theta_y=\omega_y t\).  
**Final answer**  
The flow is dense on the torus because the frequency ratio is irrational.

*Reflection:* Incommensurability guarantees ergodicity on the torus; commensurate frequencies would close the orbit after finite windings.

**Example 4 — Small perturbation of integrable system**  
*Given:* \(H=H_0(\mathbf{J})+\varepsilon V(\mathbf{J},\boldsymbol{\theta})\).  
*Find:* First-order averaged Hamiltonian.

Average over angles:  
\[
K(\mathbf{J})=\langle H\rangle_\theta=H_0(\mathbf{J})+\varepsilon\langle V\rangle_\theta.
\]
**Final answer**  
Secular Hamiltonian \(K(\mathbf{J})\) governs slow drift of the actions.

*Reflection:* The averaging step is justified only after the system has been written in action-angle coordinates; otherwise the average is ill-defined.

## 6. Common traps and how to avoid them

| Trap                              | Why it happens                                      | How to avoid it                                      |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Treating actions as Cartesian momenta | Actions are defined on a torus, not on \(\mathbb{R}^n\) | Always verify that \(\theta_i\) are periodic with period \(2\pi\). |
| Forgetting that actions are adiabatic invariants | Derivation uses only the symplectic form, not time dependence | Check that the transformation remains valid when parameters vary slowly. |
| Assuming all tori are resonant    | Most tori have irrational frequency ratios          | Compute the winding number before claiming periodicity. |
| Using the same cycle basis on every energy surface | Topology can change at critical values of the integrals | Recompute homology generators after each bifurcation. |
| Neglecting the Maslov index in semiclassical quantisation | The phase-space path acquires an extra \(\pi/2\) at each turning point | Add the correct Maslov correction when applying EBK quantisation. |
| Confusing action variables with energy | Energy is \(H(J)\), not \(J\) itself               | Keep the functional dependence \(H(J)\) explicit at every step. |
| Applying the formalism to chaotic layers | No global integrals exist                           | Verify Liouville integrability before constructing actions. |

## 7. The textbook-precise statement
Let \((M,\omega)\) be a \(2n\)-dimensional symplectic manifold and \(H\in C^\infty(M)\) a Hamiltonian possessing \(n\) independent integrals \(F_1=H,F_2,\dots,F_n\) satisfying \(\{F_i,F_j\}=0\). Suppose the common level set \(M_c=\{F_i=c_i\}\) is compact and connected. Then \(M_c\) is diffeomorphic to an \(n\)-torus, and there exist canonical coordinates \((J,\theta)\) in a neighbourhood of \(M_c\) such that the \(J_i\) are constant on \(M_c\) and the angles \(\theta_i\) satisfy
\[
\dot{\theta}_i=\omega_i(J),\qquad\omega_i=\frac{\partial H}{\partial J_i}.
\]
(Arnold, *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics*, 2nd ed., §49, Theorem 2.)

## 9. The memory technique

1. **The hook** — Picture the solar system as a set of nested hula-hoops; each hoop’s radius is an action (area swept) and a bead sliding around the hoop is the angle that ticks at constant speed.
2. **What to overlearn** — \(J_i=\frac1{2\pi}\oint p\,dq\), \(\dot\theta_i=\partial H/\partial J_i\), and the statement that \(\{J_i,J_j\}=0\).
3. **Spaced-repetition schedule** — Review the definition after 1 day, the torus construction after 3 days, the averaging theorem after 7 days, resonance conditions after 16 days, and the full EBK quantisation after 35 days.
4. **First-principles fallback** — Re-derive the actions from the closed one-form \(\mathbf{p}\cdot d\mathbf{q}\) on any cycle; the canonical condition follows automatically from Stokes’ theorem.

## 10. What this unlocks
Action-angle variables convert every subsequent perturbation calculation into a problem in Fourier analysis on tori.  

- Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem on persistence of tori  
- Nekhoroshev stability estimates  
- Averaging and adiabatic invariance theorems  
- Semiclassical quantisation (EBK)  
- Resonant normal-form theory used in accelerator and celestial mechanics
