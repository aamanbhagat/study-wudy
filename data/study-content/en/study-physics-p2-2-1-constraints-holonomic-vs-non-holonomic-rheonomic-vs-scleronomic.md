## 1. The one-sentence answer
**A constraint on a mechanical system is holonomic when it can be expressed as an integrable relation among the coordinates and possibly time, and non-holonomic otherwise; it is scleronomic when that relation does not contain time explicitly and rheonomic when it does.**

A constraint limits the possible configurations or motions of a system. In generalized coordinates \(q_i\), the simplest limits are algebraic equations that can be solved to reduce the number of independent variables. When the equation involves velocities and cannot be integrated back to a position-level relation, the reduction fails and new mathematical machinery is required.

The time dependence is independent of integrability. A rolling wheel whose contact condition never refers to the clock is scleronomic, yet still non-holonomic. A bead forced to follow a moving wire whose shape changes with time is rheonomic even though the constraint is holonomic.

> [!NOTE]
> The decisive property is integrability of the differential form that encodes the constraint: if the 1-form is exact (or can be made exact by an integrating factor), the constraint is holonomic and the system can be described on a reduced configuration manifold; otherwise the allowable velocities define a non-integrable distribution.

## 2. Why this matters — concrete and current
SpaceX Starship uses a time-varying launch-mount constraint during the first seconds after liftoff; the hold-down clamps impose a rheonomic holonomic condition that must be released at a precise instant to avoid bending moments.

Autonomous drones executing perching maneuvers on moving platforms obey non-holonomic rolling-contact constraints at touchdown; trajectory planners must therefore incorporate Pfaffian velocity constraints rather than simple position bounds.

Semiconductor wire-bonding robots employ scleronomic non-holonomic constraints when a cylindrical tool rolls without slip on a flat substrate; control algorithms based on Chow’s theorem exploit the non-holonomic accessibility to reach full configuration space with fewer actuators.

In fundamental physics, the skate on an inclined plane (Urbach, Am. J. Phys. 2021) remains a standard test-bed for vakonomic versus non-holonomic mechanics, directly affecting numerical schemes used in molecular-dynamics packages such as LAMMPS.

## 4. Building the idea — from intuition to formalism

### Step 1 — Position-level restrictions
A constraint that can be written solely in terms of the coordinates (and possibly time) immediately reduces the dimension of allowed configurations.  
Example: a particle forced to remain on a sphere gives \(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - R^2 = 0\).  
Formal statement:  
$$f(q_1,\dots,q_n,t)=0.$$  
> [!WARNING] Treating a velocity constraint as though it were already integrated will produce an incorrect reduced manifold and wrong equations of motion.

### Step 2 — Velocity-level (Pfaffian) restrictions
Many physically natural limits appear first as linear relations among velocities.  
Example: a skate that cannot slide sideways yields \(\dot x\sin\theta - \dot y\cos\theta = 0\).  
Formal statement:  
$$\sum_{i=1}^n a_i(q,t)\,\dot q_i + a_t(q,t) = 0.$$  
> [!WARNING] The coefficients \(a_i\) may depend on \(q\) and \(t\), so the equation is not automatically the total time derivative of some \(f=0\).

### Step 3 — Integrability test
A Pfaffian constraint is holonomic if and only if the differential ideal generated by the 1-form is closed (Frobenius). For a single constraint \(\omega=0\), this reduces to \(\omega\wedge d\omega=0\).  
When the test fails, no function \(f\) exists whose level set reproduces the velocity condition.  
> [!WARNING] Local integrability in patches does not imply global holonomy; topology can still obstruct reduction.

### Step 4 — Explicit time dependence
Even an integrable constraint may contain \(t\) explicitly.  
Scleronomic: \(f(q)=0\) (no \(t\)).  
Rheonomic: \(f(q,t)=0\).  
The same distinction applies to non-holonomic Pfaffian forms.  
> [!WARNING] Time dependence changes the form of the energy integral but does not alter the holonomic/non-holonomic classification.

### Step 5 — Classification table (textbook statement)

| Integrable | Time-independent | Time-dependent |
|------------|------------------|----------------|
| Yes        | Holonomic scleronomic | Holonomic rheonomic |
| No         | Non-holonomic scleronomic | Non-holonomic rheonomic |

The final rigorous statement appears in Section 7.

## 5. Worked examples — every step shown

**Example 1 — Particle on a fixed sphere**  
*Given:* Cartesian coordinates with \(x^2+y^2+z^2=R^2\).  
*Find:* Constraint type.  
Differentiate: \(x\dot x+y\dot y+z\dot z=0\). The 1-form \(\omega=x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz\) satisfies \(\omega\wedge d\omega=0\), hence integrable. No explicit \(t\), therefore holonomic scleronomic.  
**Final answer:** holonomic scleronomic.

*Reflection:* The algebraic relation already supplies the integral; differentiation merely recovers the velocity form.

**Example 2 — Skate on a plane**  
*Given:* \(\dot x\sin\theta-\dot y\cos\theta=0\).  
*Find:* Constraint type.  
The 1-form \(\omega=\sin\theta\,dx-\cos\theta\,dy\) yields \(\omega\wedge d\omega=2\cos\theta\,dx\wedge dy\wedge d\theta\neq0\), non-integrable. Coefficients independent of \(t\), therefore non-holonomic scleronomic.  
**Final answer:** non-holonomic scleronomic.

*Reflection:* The non-vanishing wedge product is the quickest diagnostic; students often forget the exterior derivative step.

**Example 3 — Bead on a moving wire**  
*Given:* Wire shape \(y=A\sin(\omega t)x\).  
*Find:* Constraint type.  
Position constraint \(f(x,y,t)=y-A\sin(\omega t)x=0\) is already integrable. Explicit \(t\) appears, therefore holonomic rheonomic.  
**Final answer:** holonomic rheonomic.

*Reflection:* Time dependence alone does not destroy holonomy once an explicit integral exists.

**Example 4 — Rolling disk with slipping allowed in one direction**  
*Given:* Vertical coin whose contact point satisfies both no-slip in the rolling direction and a time-varying incline angle \(\alpha(t)\).  
*Find:* Full classification.  
Two Pfaffian forms appear: one non-integrable (rolling) and one rheonomic (incline). The system is therefore non-holonomic rheonomic.  
**Final answer:** non-holonomic rheonomic.

*Reflection:* Multiple constraints are classified by examining each 1-form separately; the most restrictive label governs the dynamics.

## 6. Common traps and how to avoid them

| Trap | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| Assuming every velocity constraint is non-holonomic | Students forget to test \(\omega\wedge d\omega=0\). | Always compute the exterior derivative before labeling. |
| Confusing rheonomic with non-holonomic | Both involve time or velocity, yet they are orthogonal. | Separate the two binary questions: “Is it integrable?” and “Does \(t\) appear explicitly?” |
| Treating Lagrange multipliers as valid for non-holonomic cases without care | The multiplier method works, but the resulting equations are not derivable from a variational principle in the usual sense. | Use vakonomic versus non-holonomic formulations deliberately. |
| Overlooking that scleronomic constraints still allow explicit time dependence in the Lagrangian | Kinetic energy may contain \(t\) through coordinate transformations. | Check whether the constraint equation itself contains \(t\). |
| Believing holonomic constraints always reduce degrees of freedom by one | Global topology may prevent a global reduction. | Verify that the constraint defines a regular level set everywhere on the domain. |
| Applying the energy integral \(T+V=\text{const}\) to rheonomic systems | Explicit time dependence injects or removes energy. | Derive the Jacobi integral only after confirming scleronomy. |
| Ignoring quasi-coordinates when non-holonomic constraints are present | Quasi-velocities simplify the equations but hide the non-holonomic character. | Keep both quasi-velocities and the original Pfaffian forms visible. |

## 7. The textbook-precise statement
A constraint given by the Pfaffian equation \(\sum a_i(q,t)\,dq_i + a_t(q,t)\,dt=0\) is **holonomic** if there exists a function \(f(q,t)\) such that the equation is proportional to \(df=0\); otherwise it is **non-holonomic**. The same constraint is **scleronomic** when \(\partial a_i/\partial t=0\) and \(\partial a_t/\partial t=0\) identically, and **rheonomic** otherwise. (Goldstein, *Classical Mechanics*, 3rd ed., §2.4 and §2.5.)

## 9. The memory technique
**The hook:** Picture a bead on a wire (holonomic) versus a skate that refuses to slide sideways (non-holonomic). If the wire is fixed, the constraint is scleronomic; if the wire is being shaken by a motor, it becomes rheonomic.

**What to overlearn:**  
- Holonomic test: \(\omega\wedge d\omega=0\).  
- Scleronomic test: no explicit \(t\) in the constraint equation.  
- Classification table (Step 5) memorized verbatim.

**Spaced-repetition schedule:** 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 16 days, 35 days.

**First-principles fallback:** Re-derive the exterior derivative condition from the requirement that \(df=\lambda\omega\) for some integrating factor \(\lambda\), then check the coefficients for explicit time.
