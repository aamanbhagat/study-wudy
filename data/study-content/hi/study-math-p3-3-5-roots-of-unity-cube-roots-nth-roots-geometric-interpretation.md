## 1. The one-sentence answer
**Roots of unity are the solutions of the equation \(z^n = 1\) in the complex plane; they lie at the vertices of a regular n-gon inscribed in the unit circle.**

Iska matlab yeh hai ki jab aap \(z^n - 1 = 0\) solve karte ho, to aapko n distinct complex numbers milte hain jo magnitude 1 ke saath equally spaced angles par hote hain. Har root ko \(e^{2\pi i k / n}\) (k = 0,1,...,n-1) form mein likha ja sakta hai, jo De Moivre’s theorem se directly aata hai. Cube roots ke liye n=3 hota hai, jisme roots 1, \(\omega\), \(\omega^2\) kehte hain aur unka sum zero hota hai.

> [!NOTE]
> Sabse badi aha moment yeh hai ki ek polynomial equation ke roots geometrically ek perfect symmetric figure (regular polygon) bana dete hain unit circle par — algebra aur geometry ek saath dikhte hain.

## 2. Why this matters — concrete and current
In signal processing, nth roots of unity form the basis of the Discrete Fourier Transform used inside every smartphone’s FFT chip for audio and image compression at companies like Qualcomm and Apple.

In quantum computing, the controlled-phase gates and qubit phase estimation routines at IBM and Google rely on roots-of-unity angles to achieve exact constructive and destructive interference in the Bloch sphere.

Semiconductor timing circuits at TSMC and Intel use cyclotomic polynomials (which factor \(z^n-1\)) to generate precise clock phases for multi-phase sampling in high-speed SerDes links.

In orbital mechanics, NASA’s constellation phasing for satellite clusters (e.g., Starlink-like Walker constellations) places satellites at roots-of-unity angles on a common orbital circle so that relative angular velocities remain constant.

In crystallography, the symmetry groups of cubic and hexagonal lattices are generated by cube roots and sixth roots of unity; this directly determines allowed diffraction peaks observed at synchrotron facilities such as CERN’s ESRF.

## 4. Building the idea — from intuition to formalism

### Step 1 — Start with the equation itself
Aap \(z^n = 1\) likhte ho. Iska geometric matlab yeh hai ki magnitude 1 aur angle ka n-times multiple of \(2\pi\) hona chahiye.  
Example: n=1 ke liye sirf z=1.  
Formal: Solve \(z^n - 1 = 0\).  
> [!WARNING]
> Agar aap magnitude ko 1 maan liya lekin angle ko modulo \(2\pi\) nahi kiya, to aap repeated roots count kar baithoge.

### Step 2 — Write 1 in polar form
1 ko \(1 = \cos 0 + i\sin 0\) likho.  
Example: angle 0 hi hai.  
Formal: \(1 = e^{i\cdot 0}\).  
> [!WARNING]
> 0° aur 360° ko alag mat samjho; warna extra roots aa jayenge jo actually same hain.

### Step 3 — Apply De Moivre and add full rotations
General solution: \(z = \left(\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n} + i\sin\frac{2\pi k}{n}\right)\) for k=0,1,...,n-1.  
Example: n=3, k=0,1,2.  
Formal: \(z_k = e^{2\pi i k / n}\), k=0 to n-1.  
> [!WARNING]
> k ko n tak le jaane se roots repeat ho jaate hain; sirf n distinct values milti hain.

### Step 4 — Verify they satisfy the original equation
Har \(z_k^n = e^{2\pi i k} = 1\).  
Example: cube root \(\omega = e^{2\pi i /3}\), \(\omega^3=1\).  
Formal: \((e^{2\pi i k / n})^n = e^{2\pi i k} = (e^{2\pi i})^k = 1^k = 1\).

### Step 5 — Geometric placement on unit circle
Roots divide the circle into n equal arcs of \(2\pi/n\) radians.  
Example: square roots of unity at 0° and 180°.  
Formal: vertices of regular n-gon inscribed in |z|=1.

### Step 6 — Algebraic factorisation link
\(z^n-1 = (z-1)(z^{n-1} + \cdots + 1) = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(z - z_k)\).  
Example: cube roots give \(z^3-1 = (z-1)(z-\omega)(z-\omega^2)\).  
Formal statement: the cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_n(z)\) is the product over primitive roots.

## 5. Worked examples — har step show karo

**Example 1 — Cube roots of unity**  
*Given:* Solve \(z^3 = 1\).  
*Find:* All three roots.  
Step 1: Write \(1 = e^{2\pi i \cdot 0}\).  
Step 2: Roots = \(e^{2\pi i k /3}\), k=0,1,2.  
Step 3: k=0 → 1; k=1 → \(-\frac12 + i\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\); k=2 → \(-\frac12 - i\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\).  
*Why*: k values 0 se n-1 tak liye kyunki har increment se \(2\pi/3\) ka angle badhta hai.  
**Final answer**  
\[1,\quad -\dfrac12 + i\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\quad -\dfrac12 - i\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\]  
*Reflection*: Yeh example simple hai lekin yahin se symmetry ka pattern clear hota hai jo baad mein generalise hota hai.

**Example 2 — Fifth roots of unity**  
*Given:* Find all z such that \(z^5 = 1\).  
*Find:* Explicit polar and rectangular forms.  
Step 1: Angles = \(2\pi k /5\), k=0..4.  
Step 2: k=0: 1; k=1: \(\cos72^\circ + i\sin72^\circ\); etc.  
*Why*: 72° increments se regular pentagon banta hai.  
**Final answer**  
\[e^{2\pi i k /5}\ (k=0,1,2,3,4)\]  
*Reflection*: Rectangular coordinates mein irrational numbers aate hain, isliye polar form hi clean rehta hai.

**Example 3 — Sum of all nth roots**  
*Given:* Show sum of all nth roots of unity is zero.  
*Find:* Proof.  
Step 1: Roots geometric series ka sum.  
Step 2: \(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} r^k = (1-r^n)/(1-r)\) with r = e^{2\pi i /n} ≠1.  
Step 3: Numerator 1-1=0, hence sum=0.  
*Why*: Geometric series formula directly apply hota hai kyunki roots equally spaced hain.  
**Final answer**  
Sum = 0  
*Reflection*: Yeh property linear algebra mein eigenvectors ke trace se bhi linked hai.

**Example 4 — Primitive roots only**  
*Given:* Find primitive 6th roots of unity.  
*Find:* Those with order exactly 6.  
Step 1: k coprime to 6: k=1,5.  
Step 2: \(e^{2\pi i /6}\), \(e^{10\pi i /6}\).  
*Why*: gcd(k,6)=1 condition se primitive wale select hote hain.  
**Final answer**  
\[e^{2\pi i /6},\quad e^{10\pi i /6}\]  
*Reflection*: Cyclotomic polynomials exactly inhi primitive roots se bante hain.

## 6. Common traps and how to avoid them

| Trap                              | Why it happens                              | How to avoid it                              |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Forgetting k = 0 to n-1 only      | Students keep adding 2π until they get tired | Always stop at n distinct values             |
| Writing angles in degrees then mixing with radians | Calculator default mode                     | Stick to radians throughout or convert once  |
| Taking |z| ≠ 1 when solving z^n = 1 | Equation forces magnitude 1                 | Explicitly check |z_k| = 1 after calculation   |
| Counting repeated roots           | Ignoring 2π periodicity                     | Reduce angle modulo 2π before listing        |
| Confusing primitive vs all roots  | Not checking gcd(k,n)=1                     | List all roots first, then filter by order   |
| Forgetting conjugate pairs        | Odd n case mein bhi symmetry hoti hai       | Always verify if ω is root then conjugate bhi hai |

## 7. The textbook-precise statement
The nth roots of unity are the n complex numbers  
\[ \zeta_k = \exp\left(\frac{2\pi i k}{n}\right),\qquad k=0,1,\dots,n-1, \]  
which are precisely the roots of the equation \(z^n-1=0\). These points are the vertices of a regular n-gon inscribed in the unit circle centred at the origin. The minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb{Q}\) for a primitive nth root is the nth cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_n(z)\). (See Brown & Churchill, *Complex Variables and Applications*, 9e, Section 1.5.)

## 9. The memory technique
1. **The hook** — Imagine a clock whose hour marks are exactly the nth roots; the hand jumps by exactly one mark each time you multiply by a primitive root.
2. **What to overlearn** — Formula \(z_k = e^{2\pi i k / n}\) for k=0 to n-1; sum of all roots = 0; product of all roots = (−1)^{n-1}.
3. **Spaced-repetition schedule** — Review formula day 1, day 3, day 7, day 16, day 35.
4. **First-principles fallback** — Bhool jaaye to 1 ko polar form mein likho, De Moivre apply karo, aur k=0 se n-1 tak list karo.
