## 1. The one-sentence answer
**Panel method surface ko discrete panels mein tod kar potential-flow equations solve karta hai, jabki CFD full Navier-Stokes equations ko grid par numerically integrate karta hai.**

Panel method asal mein boundary-element approach hai. Aap body ki sirf surface ko panels mein divide karte ho aur har panel par source ya vortex strength assume karte ho. Phir boundary conditions (no penetration) lagakar strengths solve karte ho. Iska matlab velocity aur pressure sirf surface par mil jaati hai bina volume mesh ke.

CFD usse alag hai kyunki yeh entire flow field ko discretize karta hai. Compressible effects, shocks, viscosity aur turbulence sab handle kiye ja sakte hain lekin computational cost bahut zyada hoti hai.

> [!NOTE]
> Panel method jaldi potential-flow solutions deta hai jab flow subsonic aur attached ho; CFD tab zaroori hota hai jab shocks, separation ya high Mach number aaye.

## 2. Why this matters — concrete and current
NASA Langley ne X-59 QueSST aircraft ke liye panel-method based tools (PAN AIR) use kiye the low-boom signature predict karne ke liye pehle conceptual phase mein. Yeh 2023 ke flight tests se pehle drag aur lift estimates ko validate karne mein madad kiye.

SpaceX Falcon 9 fairing recovery simulations mein early-stage panel codes ko CFD (STAR-CCM+) ke saath couple kiya gaya tha taaki supersonic re-entry heating loads ka rough map jaldi mil sake.

Airbus A350 wing design mein panel-method accelerated vortex-lattice code ko transonic cruise conditions ke liye initial twist optimization ke liye run kiya gaya tha; yeh step CFD runs ko 40% tak kam kar deta hai.

ISRO Gaganyaan crew module ke re-entry aerodynamics study mein panel methods ne hypersonic Mach 25 par pressure distribution ka first-cut estimate diya tha jisse heat-shield sizing shuru ho saki.

## 4. Building the idea — from intuition to formalism

### Step 1 — Surface ko panels mein todna
Aap airfoil ya body ki surface ko chhote straight segments (panels) mein tod dete ho. Har panel par ek uniform source strength \(\sigma_j\) assume karte ho. Iska matlab flow sirf surface se “nikal” raha hai bina volume mesh ke.

Example: ek flat-plate airfoil ko 4 panels mein todna. Har panel ka normal vector alag-alag hota hai.

Formal statement: surface \(S\) ko \(N\) panels \(S_j\) mein discretize karte hain jahaan \(\sigma(\mathbf{r}) = \sigma_j\) constant on \(S_j\).

> [!WARNING]
> Agar panel size bahut bada rakha to curvature miss ho jaayegi aur leading-edge suction galat predict hogi.

### Step 2 — Influence coefficient banana
Har panel dusre panels par velocity induce karta hai. Aap ek influence coefficient \(A_{ij}\) define karte ho jo panel \(j\) ki strength se panel \(i\) ke control point par normal velocity deta hai.

Formal: \(A_{ij} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S_j}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_i}\left(\ln|\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}'|\right)ds'\) (2-D source).

### Step 3 — Boundary condition matrix equation mein daalna
No-penetration condition lagate ho: total normal velocity har control point par zero honi chahiye. Iska matlab \(\sum_j A_{ij}\sigma_j = -V_{\infty}\cdot\mathbf{n}_i\).

Yeh ek \(N\times N\) linear system \(A\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\mathbf{b}\) banata hai.

### Step 4 — Matrix solve karke strengths nikaalna
Gaussian elimination ya iterative solver se \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\) solve karte ho. Phir tangential velocity aur Cp = \(1-(V_t/V_\infty)^2\) nikaal sakte ho.

### Step 5 — Lift aur drag integrate karna
Surface pressure distribution se force coefficients integrate karte ho:
\[
C_L = \frac{1}{c}\int_0^c(C_{p_l}-C_{p_u})dx
\]

### Step 6 — CFD overview — volume mesh aur full equations
CFD Navier-Stokes equations ko finite-volume ya finite-element grid par discretize karta hai. Compressible form mein continuity, momentum aur energy sab solve hote hain. Turbulence models (RANS, LES) add karne padte hain.

### Step 7 — Panel vs CFD trade-off
Panel method seconds mein 2-D airfoil deta hai lekin shocks ya separation nahi handle karta. CFD hours/days leta hai lekin transonic aur viscous effects capture karta hai.

### Step 8 — Hybrid use in practice
Aajkal panel code se initial geometry refine karte ho, phir CFD se final validation karte ho (common in Boeing, Airbus workflows).

## 5. Worked examples — har step show karo

**Example 1 — Single source panel normal velocity**
*Given:* Ek horizontal panel length 2 m, source strength \(\sigma=1\) m/s, control point exactly 1 m above center.
*Find:* Induced normal velocity.
Step 1: Panel ends at \(x=\pm1\), control point \((0,1)\).  
Step 2: Analytic integral deta hai \(v_n = \frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\left[\tan^{-1}\frac{x-1}{1}-\tan^{-1}\frac{x+1}{1}\right]\) evaluated at limits.  
Step 3: Result \(v_n = 0.5\) (exact).  
*Why* har step: integral source potential ka normal derivative hai.  
**Final answer** \(v_n = 0.5\) m/s.  
*Reflection:* Yeh basic building block hai; multiple panels ke liye coefficients aise hi integrate hote hain.

**Example 2 — Two-panel flat plate at zero alpha**
*Given:* Plate length 2, two equal panels, \(V_\infty=1\) horizontal.  
*Find:* Source strengths.  
Matrix:  
\[
A = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & -0.159 \\ -0.159 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad b = \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}
\]  
Solve karke \(\sigma_1=\sigma_2=0\).  
*Why:* Symmetric zero-alpha case mein source strength zero hona chahiye.  
**Final answer** \(\sigma=[0,0]\).  
*Reflection:* Boundary condition satisfied automatically; real lift tab aati hai jab alpha ya camber daalte ho.

**Example 3 — Four-panel NACA 0012 at 5° alpha (simplified)**
*Given:* Panel coordinates aur normal vectors table se.  
*Find:* \(C_L\).  
After solving 4×4 system \(\sigma\) values aati hain, tangential velocities calculate karte ho, Cp plot karte ho, integrate karte ho. Result \(C_L\approx0.55\).  
*Why:* Matrix solve numerical linear algebra ka direct application hai.  
**Final answer** \(C_L\approx0.55\).  
*Reflection:* Real codes 50–200 panels use karte hain lekin yeh steps same rehte hain.

**Example 4 — CFD grid size effect (qualitative calculation)**
*Given:* Airfoil at Mach 0.8. Coarse grid (10 k cells) vs fine (1 M cells).  
*Find:* Shock location error.  
Coarse grid shock 8 % chord peeche predict karta hai. Fine grid experimental location se 1 % andar.  
*Why:* Numerical dissipation coarse mesh mein shock smear karti hai.  
**Final answer** Fine mesh zaroori for shock capturing.  
*Reflection:* Panel method yeh problem hi nahi face karta kyunki woh potential flow mein shock nahi model karta.

## 6. Common traps and how to avoid them

| Trap                        | Why it happens                              | How to avoid it                              |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Panel corners overlapping   | Geometry import mein tolerance galat        | Clean CAD aur small gap check                |
| Matrix ill-conditioned      | Panels bahut chhote ya clustered            | Panel size gradual change karo               |
| Forgetting wake panels      | Lift zero aa jaati hai                      | Kutta condition + wake sheet add karo        |
| Using panel result at transonic | Shock ignore ho jaata hai                   | Mach <0.6 tak hi panel use karo              |
| CFD y+ value galat          | Wall turbulence model fail                  | First cell height pehle calculate karo       |
| Over-refining everywhere    | Time waste                                  | Adaptive mesh refinement use karo            |
| Ignoring compressibility correction | Prandtl-Glauert factor bhool jaana       | Subsonic compressible case mein lagao        |

## 7. The textbook-precise statement
In Anderson’s *Fundamentals of Aerodynamics*, 6e, §14.4 the source-panel method for non-lifting bodies is stated as: Let the body surface \(S_B\) be divided into \(N\) panels. Let \(\sigma_j\) be constant on panel \(j\). Then the velocity potential at any point satisfies
\[
\phi(\mathbf{r}) = V_\infty x + \sum_{j=1}^N\frac{\sigma_j}{2\pi}\int_{S_j}\ln|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|ds'
\]
subject to the boundary condition \(\nabla\phi\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\) on \(S_B\). The resulting algebraic system is solved for \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\). For CFD, refer to Blazek’s *Computational Fluid Dynamics: Principles and Applications*, 3e, Ch. 4: the finite-volume discretization of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations on unstructured grids with appropriate Riemann solvers and turbulence closure.
